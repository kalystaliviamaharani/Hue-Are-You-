Hue are you?

Colour plays a powerful role in how we perceive brands. It can influence emotions, shape buying decisions, and even define a brand’s personality before a single word is read. Whether you're building a brand from scratch or refreshing an existing one, choosing the right colour is key.

Psychologists and marketers have long studied the impact of colour on human behaviour. In branding, each colour carries its own emotional weight and meaning. Brands like Coca-Cola, Spotify, and Cadbury don’t just use colour — they own it.

This website breaks down the psychology behind five of the most commonly used branding colours. Whether you’re launching a startup, designing a logo, or just curious about colour theory, this guide will help you understand what each hue communicates — and how it can strengthen your brand identity.

Explore each section to see how colour shapes perception, emotion, and connection. Let’s find the perfect colour that not only looks good, but feels right for your brand.

Looking for something bold?

RED

The Colour of Power and Passion

Red is one of the most emotionally intense colours. It evokes strong feelings — love, passion, anger, urgency. Brands use red to demand attention, stimulate appetite, and create excitement.

Psychologically, red increases heart rate and blood pressure, making it a favourite in marketing when urgency or impulse is the goal. Think sales banners, clearance signs, or “Buy Now” buttons — red triggers quick decisions.

In branding, red communicates boldness and confidence. Brands like Coca-Cola, YouTube, Netflix, and LEGO use red to project energy, passion, and approachability.

But red can also be overwhelming if overused. It’s best used in moderation or in combination with calmer tones. In web design, red often serves as a call-to-action colour, drawing the eye quickly without overstaying its welcome.

Used well, red energizes, motivates, and ignites strong feelings — all vital in building a memorable brand presence.

Looking for something exciting?

YELLOW

The Colour of Optimism and Attention

Yellow is the colour of sunlight, joy, and youthfulness. It naturally evokes happiness and optimism, making it a powerful tool in brand identity when used with care.

Psychologically, yellow is associated with warmth, clarity, and confidence. It’s also the most visible colour from a distance, which is why it's used for warning signs and school buses. In branding, it grabs attention in a way that’s friendly and non-threatening.

Brands like IKEA, McDonald’s, and Snapchat use yellow to promote a sense of fun and approachability. It suggests energy without being aggressive. Tech brands like Ferrari (in its classic yellow shield) and National Geographic use yellow to symbolize prestige, curiosity, and adventure.

However, yellow can also trigger anxiety or strain the eyes if overused. On websites, it’s often used as a highlight or accent, not as the main background colour.

In branding, yellow says: “We’re here, we’re fun, and we’re confident.” It’s bold without being brash — perfect for youth-oriented or innovative brands.

Looking for something stable?

GREEN

The Colour of Growth and Balance

Green is nature’s colour — a symbol of growth, health, and harmony. It’s often used in branding to convey freshness, sustainability, and trust.

Psychologically, green has a balancing and calming effect. It sits between warm and cool tones, making it a neutral, reassuring presence. It’s linked to renewal, wealth, and healing, which is why it’s common in health and finance sectors.

Brands like Starbucks, Whole Foods, Spotify, and Land Rover use green to promote ideas of eco-friendliness, relaxation, or reliability. In finance, green often relates to money and stability — like Fidelity or TD Bank.

Green is also widely used by sustainable or organic brands, where environmental values are key. It signals ethical choices, natural products, and a sense of responsibility.

In web design, green is often used in confirmation buttons (“Submit”, “Success”) due to its calming and positive associations. It encourages users to move forward with confidence. Green is the brand colour of balance — connecting the digital world with our human need for peace and progress.

Looking for something reliable?

BLUE

The Colour of Trust and Intelligence

Blue is the most widely liked colour in the world — and one of the most popular in branding. It symbolizes trust, stability, and professionalism.

Psychologically, blue slows the heart rate and creates a sense of calm. It’s associated with logic, clarity, and focus, which is why it’s the go-to for corporate, tech, and financial industries.

Brands like Facebook, Twitter, Samsung, Intel, and PayPal all lean into blue to emphasize their reliability and intelligence. Even government and healthcare institutions use blue for the same reason — think the NHS, UN, or LinkedIn.

Darker blues project seriousness and strength, while lighter shades suggest friendliness and modernity. In UX/UI design, blue is often used for navigation bars, backgrounds, or trusted links.

The downside? Blue can also feel cold or impersonal if not balanced with warm tones or human elements.

In branding, blue says: “You can trust us.” It’s the colour of calm confidence — and in today’s world, that’s worth its weight in gold.

Looking for something imaginative?

PURPLE

The Colour of Creativity and Luxury

Purple blends the passion of red and the calm of blue — making it the colour of creativity, imagination, and mystery. Historically, purple was rare and expensive to produce, so it became associated with royalty, wealth, and spirituality.

Psychologically, purple stimulates problem-solving, daydreaming, and innovation. It’s often used in branding to signal luxury, uniqueness, or artistic flair.

Brands like Cadbury, Hallmark, and Twitch use purple to stand out and feel elevated. It’s also popular with beauty, tech, and artistic brands that want to evoke a sense of high-end exclusivity or creative edge.

Lighter shades (lavender, lilac) feel calming and gentle — great for self-care or wellness brands. Darker purples feel more opulent and mysterious, ideal for luxury goods or innovative startups.

In digital design, purple is less common — so when used, it often pops. It works well for thematic sites, streaming platforms, and entertainment brands that want to feel both modern and magical.

Purple is for brands that dare to dream differently — those who want to be remembered for more than just a product.
